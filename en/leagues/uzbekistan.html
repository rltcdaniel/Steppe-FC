Context

The Uzbekistan Super League is the country's top football division, played since 1992. Unlike Kazakhstan, Uzbekistan competes in AFC (Asian) competitions: the champion enters the AFC Champions League Elite, with other high finishers going into the AFC Champions League Two.

Pakhtakor Tashkent are the club with the most league titles in history. Reigning champions are Neftchi Fergana, who successfully defended their 2025 title and are comfortably leading this season's table.

Since the 2026 season, squads are allowed 4 foreign players, one of whom must be from Asia and three from the rest of the world — a key rule for intermediation purposes. In August 2025 the Uzbek government approved an official plan to digitalize and popularize football, explicitly aiming to support Uzbek players abroad.

The league is broadcast domestically on NTRCU Sport.

As of this update we haven't identified any Spanish player currently active in this league — there are several South Americans (Argentine, Chilean), suggesting the league is already open to Spanish-speaking signings, but with no Spanish presence yet.

Teams

  • Neftchi FerganaFergana

    Founded in 1962, nicknamed "the Tigers"; financed by the Fergana oil refinery. Six-time champions of Uzbekistan, and reigning champions after defending their 2025 title.

    Wikipedia (EN) Sofascore Official site
  • PakhtakorTashkent

    Founded in 1956, by far the most successful club in Uzbekistan. Their name means "cotton grower", reflecting the country's agricultural heritage.

    Wikipedia (EN) Sofascore Official site
  • NavbahorNamangan

    Founded in 1978, based in Namangan, in the heart of the Fergana Valley. Their name means "new spring"; one of only three clubs to have played every season of the league since independence.

    Wikipedia (EN) Sofascore Official site
  • BuxoroBukhara

    Club from the historic city of Bukhara, one of the oldest cities in Central Asia.

    Wikipedia (EN) Sofascore
  • OKMKOlmaliq

    Founded in 2004, owned by the Olmaliq mining and metallurgical complex (hence the initials); nicknamed "the Miners".

    Wikipedia (EN) Sofascore
  • LokomotivTashkent

    Founded in 2002, owned by Uzbek Railways.

    Wikipedia (EN) Sofascore Official site
  • AndijonAndijon

    Club from the city of Andijan, at the eastern edge of the Fergana Valley.

    Sofascore
  • Dinamo SamarqandSamarkand

    One of Uzbekistan's oldest clubs, founded in 1960; has changed name numerous times throughout its history.

    Wikipedia (EN) Sofascore Official site
  • NasafQarshi

    Founded in 1986 in Qarshi, nicknamed "the Dragons"; one of the country's leading clubs over the last two decades.

    Wikipedia (EN) Sofascore Official site
  • SogdianaJizzax

    Founded in 1970 in Jizzakh, named after the ancient historical region of Sogdia.

    Wikipedia (EN) Sofascore Official site
  • QizilqumNavoiy

    Club from Navoiy, in the centre of the country, near the Kyzylkum desert the team is named after.

    Sofascore
  • SurkhonTermiz

    Founded in 1968 in Termez, a border city with Afghanistan at Uzbekistan's southern tip.

    Wikipedia (EN) Sofascore
  • XorazmUrganch

    Represents Urgench, in the historic Khorezm region, by the delta of the Amu Darya river.

    Sofascore
  • Kokand 1912Kokand

    Club from the historic city of Kokand; the "1912" in the name points back to the club's origins in football under the Russian Empire.

    Sofascore
  • BunyodkorTashkent

    Founded in 2005 in Tashkent as a high-ambition project: once signed Rivaldo and hired Zico and Luiz Felipe Scolari as coaches.

    Wikipedia (EN) Sofascore
  • Mash'alMuborak

    Represents Muborak, a town tied to the natural gas industry, in the Kashkadarya region.

    Sofascore

Fun fact: several clubs have played part of the season as home side in a different city (Sogdiana in Zomin, Mash'al in G'uzor, Navbahor in Tashkent until matchday 15) — the same phenomenon seen in Kazakhstan.
