Scout's Report

SFCPlaying-style analysis — Steppe FC Scouting

FotMob notes, even in his brief, low-key spell in India, that he "scores highly on goals and minutes compared to midfielders" — a pattern repeated throughout his career and one that defines him better than any generic "midfielder" tag: 14 goals in 50 games for Real Zaragoza B, and 5 in 47 for Badalona Futur, figures well above what you'd expect from a holding interior. He's not a fixed-position pivot but a midfielder with late arrivals into the box, exploiting the second phase of an attack more than the first line of build-up. Trained at Barcelona's La Masia and with a youth spell at Wolverhampton's reserve side, he carries a technical grounding and reading of space above the average of the Spanish regional tiers he's competed in since — visible in the fact that, at nearly every club he's played for, he ranks among the side's most offensively involved players. The counterpoint to his career is uneven standards between clubs and a notable lack of continuity — few spells of more than one full season, a pattern worth watching as he settles into Kyrgyz football, where he's now directly competing for the title with Bars.

Biography

Albert Torras Crespo played for Penya Barcelonista Sant Celoni, RCD Espanyol and, from 2011, FC Barcelona's La Masia. In 2013, aged just 17, he moved to England to sign for Wolverhampton Wanderers, then in the Championship.

Back in Spain he built a well-travelled career across regional football and Spain's fourth tier: Málaga, Zaragoza (making his first-team debut in 2019), Ejea, Lleida, Calahorra, Badalona Futur and Olot. In 2025 he tried his luck in India, with Gokulam Kerala in the I-League, without making a first-team appearance, and in February 2026 he signed for Bars, Kyrgyzstan's reigning champions.

He is, along with Palma-born Munir Abdeselam and fellow Mallorcan Ángel Sánchez Baró, one of only three Spanish footballers to have ever played in the Kyrgyz league — and the only Catalan of the three.
