Spanish Players

We've found five Spanish footballers currently active in Central Asia's leagues, split between Kazakhstan and Kyrgyzstan — plus two coaches. In Uzbekistan, Turkmenistan and Tajikistan, for now, there are none. Here's each of them: who they are, where they've played, and where they are today.

Marc Gual

Kazakhstan · Kairat Almaty

Forward · 1.84 m · born 13 March 1996 in Badalona (Barcelona)

Marc Gual Huguet came through the youth ranks at FC Barcelona and RCD Espanyol before a professional career built on loan spells: Real Zaragoza, Girona and Real Madrid Castilla, without settling anywhere, until he found a regular run of games at Alcorcón.

In 2022 he moved abroad for the first time, first to Ukraine (Dnipro-1, with the league disrupted by the war) and then to Poland, where he had his best spell as a professional: two and a half seasons at Legia Warsaw, one of the country's biggest clubs. After a year at Portugal's Rio Ave, he signed in 2026 for Kairat Almaty, Kazakhstan's reigning champions and the team that knocked out Celtic in Champions League qualifying 2025-26.

With Kairat he has gone straight into the Champions League league phase, sharing a dressing room with players such as Belarusian Alyaksandr Martynovich and Brazilian João Paulo.

Pablo Álvarez

Kazakhstan · Aktobe

Midfielder · 1.85 m · born 23 April 1997 in Langreo (Asturias)

Pablo Álvarez García came through Villarreal's academy and played for Alavés's reserve side before leaving Spain in 2021 to sign for Bulgaria's Cherno More, where he became a regular starter over several seasons.

After spells in Croatia (Rijeka) and Ukraine (Karpaty Lviv), he signed in 2026 for Kazakhstan's Aktobe, one of the league's historic clubs and, along with FK Atyrau, one of only two named directly after their city.

Agustín "Pibe" Pastoriza

Kazakhstan · Aktobe

Winger · 1.77 m · born 31 January 1996 in Adrogué (Argentina) · dual Argentine-Spanish national

Agustín Emanuel Pastoriza Cacabelos, known as "Pibe", was born in Argentina but moved with his family to Galicia at just five years old, to Cambados, his mother's hometown. His entire football education is Spanish: he came through Pontevedra and made his La Liga debut with Real Betis in 2014, aged just 18.

His adult career has played out almost entirely in Galician regional football (Pontevedra, Ourense, Arenteiro) and Spain's fourth tier (Cultural Leonesa), with a brief spell in Finland along the way. In January 2026 he signed for Kazakhstan's Aktobe, where he now lines up alongside his (adopted) compatriot Pablo Álvarez.

Albert Torras

Kyrgyzstan · Bars

Midfielder · 1.83 m · born 13 June 1996 in Vallgorguina (Barcelona)

Albert Torras Crespo played for Penya Barcelonista Sant Celoni, RCD Espanyol and, from 2011, FC Barcelona's La Masia. In 2013, aged just 17, he moved to England to sign for Wolverhampton Wanderers, then in the Championship.

Back in Spain he built a well-travelled career across regional football and Spain's fourth tier: Málaga, Zaragoza (making his first-team debut in 2019), Ejea, Lleida, Calahorra, Badalona Futur and Olot. In 2025 he tried his luck in India, with Gokulam Kerala in the I-League, without making a first-team appearance, and in 2026 he signed for Bars, Kyrgyzstan's reigning champions.

Munir Abdeselam El Handi

Kyrgyzstan · Alga Bishkek

Defender (centre-back / left-back) · born 2 July 1998

Trained in Balearic football (Penya Independent, CE Andratx), Munir — also spelled Monir — Abdeselam El Handi played in Spain's regional leagues and had a brief spell at Extremadura's CD Montijo before becoming, in 2025, the first Spanish footballer to play in the Kyrgyz league, signing for FC Talant.

A year later he changed clubs without changing country: he joined Alga Bishkek, one of the capital's historic clubs, where he still plays. In his own words, after finishing his first season in Kyrgyzstan: "far from home, yet I have felt at home here."

Spanish dugouts

Beyond the players, there's Spanish coaching presence in the region too. Catalan coach Francesc Bonet manages Bars, Kyrgyzstan's reigning champions and Albert Torras's club. In Tajikistan, Andalusian coach Juan Cortés started the season in charge of CSKA Dushanbe, though we haven't been able to confirm whether he's still in the role as of this update.

In Kazakhstan we've also spotted a goalkeeper, Yan Andrin, from Langreo (Asturias) like Pablo Álvarez, on Kaspiy Aktau's squad list — but with such a thin public footprint (no confirmed minutes or matchday squads) that we'd rather not treat him as a full profile until we can confirm it more solidly.

Where there's no Spanish presence yet

Uzbekistan: no Spanish player is currently active in the Super League. There was one in recent seasons, and a club (Dinamo Samarqand) signed a centre-back named Salvador Sánchez in February 2026 — but he's Argentine, not Spanish, and has already left the club.

Tajikistan: no Spanish player in its league, which Istiqlol dominate almost single-handedly. There has, as noted above, been Spanish presence on the bench.

Turkmenistan: no Spanish player or coach at all. The country's closed nature and political particularities make it hard for foreign professionals of any origin to arrive.
