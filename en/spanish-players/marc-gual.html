Biography

Marc Gual Huguet came through the youth ranks at FC Barcelona and RCD Espanyol before a professional career built on loan spells: Real Zaragoza, Girona and Real Madrid Castilla, without settling anywhere, until he found a regular run of games at Alcorcón.

In 2022 he moved abroad for the first time, first to Ukraine (Dnipro-1, with the league disrupted by the war) and then to Poland, where he had his best spell as a professional: two and a half seasons at Legia Warsaw, one of the country's biggest clubs. After a year at Portugal's Rio Ave, he signed in 2026 for Kairat Almaty, Kazakhstan's reigning champions and the team that knocked out Celtic in Champions League qualifying 2025-26.

With Kairat he has gone straight into the 2026-27 Champions League league phase.

A reference penalty-box striker, right-footed and 1.84 m per Sofascore, whose finishing profile is unusual for the role. The most revealing data point on his FotMob performance graph is the contrast between ball involvement (91st percentile for touches) and shooting intensity (99th for shot attempts): he isn't a '9' who waits inside the six-yard box, but a forward with real range who links into the wider attacking structure, constantly occupies the last defensive line, and manufactures his own shot rather than depending on crosses or through-balls from a teammate.

His aerial duels won figure (15th percentile), however, is the flip side: he isn't a heading threat or a physical focal point, which is relevant because it limits his value in systems built around long balls or direct play. His scoring record — top scorer of the Ekstraklasa in 2022/23 with 16 goals, a brace in the Conference League against Dinamo Minsk in 2024 — confirms his finishing instinct works just as well with the ball at his feet, inside or outside the box.

His Sofascore monthly rating curve over the last year (from 6.3 in his final Portuguese spell to peaks of 7.0-7.6 already in Kazakhstan) traces a sustained performance improvement since joining Kairat, not an isolated spike.

The low defensive-actions figure (26th percentile) is unsurprising for a focal-point striker, but it does shape the kind of set-up he performs best in: teams that dominate possession, generate positional overloads and feed him in behind, rather than systems built on sustained high pressing and quick recoveries in the opponent's half. In Kairat's context, a side used to having the ball and playing continental football, that profile fits almost naturally.
