About

Steppe FC was born in 2026 out of a simple observation: football in Central Asia — Kazakhstan, Uzbekistan, Turkmenistan, Kyrgyzstan and Tajikistan — barely gets any coverage in Spanish, even though more and more is happening there worth writing about. We're an independent project, with no ties to any club or federation, combining data-driven coverage of the region with the longer-term goal of becoming a real bridge between Spanish talent and its clubs.

What you'll find here

  • Blog

    Analysis, standout results and the stories that explain why these five leagues are worth following: from Kairat Almaty's knockout of Celtic to the meteoric rise of Turkmenistan's Arkadag.

  • Leagues & data

    Standings, teams and context for each federation, updated as often as we can and always citing where each figure comes from: Sofascore, Wikipedia and official federations.

  • Bridge to Spain

    The medium-term goal: acting as intermediaries between Spanish players and Central Asian clubs. Right now there isn't a single active Spanish player in any of the five leagues — that's the opportunity.

Why Central Asia

Central Asian football has been gaining ground for years without much notice in Europe. Kairat Almaty knocked Celtic out in Champions League qualifying 2025-26, becoming the easternmost club ever to play the competition's league phase. Kazakhstan, Uzbekistan, Kyrgyzstan and Tajikistan have all expanded their leagues to more teams this very season. And in Turkmenistan, the newly founded Arkadag are racking up titles with a dominance reminiscent of other regional "state clubs" in their golden years.

With all that movement, it's striking how unexplored the ground still is for Spanish talent: across all five leagues, we haven't identified a single active Spanish player so far. There is a growing South American and African presence, which suggests these clubs are already open to signing beyond their immediate surroundings. Steppe FC wants to be the one building that specific bridge.

How we work

  • 1

    Verified, sourced data. Every standings table and team profile states where the figure comes from — Sofascore, Wikipedia or the club's official site — so it can be checked and, if it changes, updated.

  • 2

    Bilingual from day one. All content exists in both Spanish and English, aimed at Spanish-speaking fans as much as at the region's own clubs and agents.

  • 3

    A project under construction. We're independent and just getting started: we'd rather move slowly with correct data than rush to fill in sections.
